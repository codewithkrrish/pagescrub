You came for a pancake recipe. What you got instead was a fifteen-paragraph memoir about somebody's grandmother, three autoplaying videos, a newsletter modal, and a cookie banner with forty-two toggles. Somewhere in the middle, possibly, there are pancakes.

This is not an accident. Every one of those interruptions is there because it converts — a fraction of a percent of visitors click, and at web scale a fraction of a percent pays salaries. The cost is pushed onto you: your time, your attention, your scrolling thumb.

Reader modes help, but they live inside the browser chrome and most people never find them. Bookmarklets are the older, scrappier answer: one click, no install, no account, and the page just… calms down.

That's the whole idea behind pagescrub. It doesn't try to be clever about extracting article text like a reader mode does — it just removes the stuff that was never the article: the overlays, the sticky chrome, the scroll locks. What remains is the page as the author (probably) intended it.

There are limits, of course. A paywall that withholds the text on the server can't be scrubbed away — no client-side trick can fetch what was never sent. And sites that re-inject popups as you scroll will need a second click. Honest tools admit what they can't do.

Anyway. The pancakes: flour, eggs, milk, a pinch of salt, a hot pan. You didn't need the memoir. Nobody did.
